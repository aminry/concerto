This is a standard separation; the Linux Foundation, the Mozilla Foundation, and many other open-source projects use the same pattern.

This policy is intended to be friendly. Almost every reasonable use of the name is permitted. The rules exist mainly to prevent user confusion about what is and isn't an official Concerto release.

## Permitted nominative use — no permission needed

You can use the name "Concerto" without asking when:

- **Factually describing interoperability or compatibility.** "This plugin works with Concerto." "An MCP server for use with Concerto." "Imports/exports the Concerto workspace format."
- **Comparison and review.** "Concerto vs. [other tool]." Honest reviews, blog posts, tutorials, conference talks, books, and academic papers about Concerto.
- **Linking to the official project or documentation.** Hyperlinks to `concerto.app` or this repository.
- **Stating that your fork is a fork of Concerto.** "ForkName, a community fork of Concerto." The new project must use a different primary name.
- **Inside source files.** Symbol names (`ConcertoConfig`, `concerto_link_pr`, etc.) and identifiers that are necessary for compatibility are fine.

For these uses, do not modify the Concerto logo, do not imply Concerto Inc endorses you, and do not use the name as the primary identifier of a product you ship.

## Uses that require permission

Please email **trademarks@concerto.app** before doing any of the following:

- **Distributing a binary, package, container image, or App Store / Play Store listing under the "Concerto" name.** Including the official builds is the operational role of Concerto Inc; redistributing identical or near-identical binaries under the same name causes user confusion.
- **Selling merchandise** featuring the Concerto name or logo.
- **Using the Concerto logo unmodified** in marketing materials, especially in a way that implies official affiliation.
- **Registering domains, social-media handles, package names** (`pip install concerto`, `brew install concerto`, etc.), or app-store names that would lead users to assume official Concerto distribution.

For corporate use cases (CI/CD pipelines mentioning Concerto, internal tooling integrations, partner directory listings, etc.), an informal email is usually all that's needed.

## What forks should do

You are absolutely free to fork this repository under the MIT License. To keep things clear for users:

- **Pick a new name** for the fork's distribution. The codebase can still mention Concerto internally as much as needed for compatibility.
- **Don't ship App Store / Play Store apps** under the Concerto name. The official mobile clients are tied to Concerto Inc's developer accounts.
- **Don't use the Concerto logo** as your project's primary identifier.

The MIT License protects your right to use, modify, and redistribute the code. This policy protects users' ability to know what is and isn't an official build.

## Questions

Email trademarks@concerto.app. We aim to reply within five business days. We are not in the business of pursuing trademark disputes — most of these can be settled with a quick email.
